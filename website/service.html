A new cobot task in a week.

One task, five working days, one fixed price agreed before we start. For system integrators who want to quote automation jobs faster, and high-mix manufacturers who want their cobot doing more than one job.

The week

What happens, day by day.

Day 1 · MonShow the taskA short call about the cell, the parts and the cycle. Your operator films the task, or we film it with them on site.
Day 2 · TueLabel and describeWe turn the video into labelled steps and a task file, then review it with you.
Day 3 · WedGenerate and simulateThe robot program is generated and checked in simulation with your cell layout.
Day 4 · ThuRun on your cellFirst runs at reduced speed with an engineer present. We tune positions and timing.
Day 5 · FriHand overWorking program, task file, demonstration video and short notes for your team.
What you get

Everything your team needs to keep it running.

  • A native robot program (URScript for UR cobots), commented step by step and checked in simulation.
  • The task file, so the program can be regenerated when a location or part changes.
  • The demonstration video and the labelled steps, as a record of how the task is done.
  • A one-page handover with what was tested, known limits and how to change common things.
  • Optional monthly support per cell, for changes and new variants.
Good fits

Tasks that are slow to program the usual way.

We focus where teaching by demonstration saves the most time. If a task is simpler to program by hand, we will say so.

First

Kitting

Collecting different parts into trays or kits, with slots, layers and changing part mixes.

First

Pick-and-place

Moving parts between fixtures, conveyors and trays, especially when the parts or places vary.

Next

Machine tending

Loading and unloading a machine, waiting for its door and cycle signals, and repeating.

A good fit when

  • The task changes often or comes in many variants
  • You already have a cobot, or plan to buy one
  • An operator can show the task by hand

Not a fit (yet) when

  • The task is simple and fixed: normal programming is cheaper
  • It needs force control, such as tight assembly or polishing
  • The cell has no safety assessment or cannot run at reduced speed

Which robots does it work with?

Universal Robots e-Series cobots (UR3e, UR5e, UR10e) first, with URScript programs. FANUC CRX and Doosan are planned. Tell us what you have.

Do we need new hardware?

No new robot. You need the cobot, a gripper it can switch with a digital output, and a camera or phone to film the task.

How is safety handled?

Programs run in simulation first, then at reduced speed with an engineer present. The cell's risk assessment and safety settings stay with you or your integrator.

How much does it cost?

A fixed price per task, agreed before we start, based on the task and the cell. Ask us for a quote with a short description of the task.

Is this ready for production?

Not yet. The software is a working prototype verified in simulation. Pilot projects are how we take it onto real cells, together with partners who know that.
