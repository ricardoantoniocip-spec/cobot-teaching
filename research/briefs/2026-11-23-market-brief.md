# Market brief 2026-11-23 (16 - 22 Nov 2026)

## What matters for you this week
- **Genesis AI (Paris and California) raised a $500M Series A at $3.5B. It captures workers' hand and finger movements with cameras and gloves, plus touch, to train its robots.** That is your sensor-sleeve idea, at scale, with European money (Bpifrance joined). They build their own robots (Eno) and rent them out, so they won't program the UR or FANUC cobot a customer already owns. That gap is still yours, but don't pitch "gloves to teach robots" as new.
- **Human-demonstration data is now a funded category of its own.** Humyn Labs raised a $47M Series A for kits that record people doing jobs (sight, sound, motion, touch) and turn the recordings into training data. Together with GenRobot ($100M, 2 Nov), investors now pay for the capture side. Your sleeve plus a verified cobot program is a narrower, deployable version.
- **No news for Portugal or the Netherlands, and no new grant calls that fit you.** Not new this week, but missed earlier: Teradyne is reported to pair Universal Robots arms with Sereact's Cortex AI (9 Nov, second-hand source). If confirmed, UR itself is moving toward AI that needs less programming. Check it before using it in a pitch.

## Implications
1. **Sleeve positioning:** the sleeve alone is not a moat (see the patent check too). Your edge is the output: a checked native program on the customer's existing cobot, delivered in a week, at a fixed price.
2. **New interview question:** "If a vendor rented you a robot that learns from gloves, would you take it, or do you want your current cobot reprogrammed?" The answer tests your core bet.
3. **Pitch:** Genesis, Humyn and GenRobot together are useful proof that "learning from human demonstration" attracts money. Cite them as market validation, not as rivals you can out-raise.
